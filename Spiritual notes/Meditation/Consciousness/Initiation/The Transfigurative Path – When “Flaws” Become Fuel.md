
There comes a moment on every seeker’s path when the old way of growing no longer works. The way, where one walks around carrying guilt, shame, and self‑reproach for every personal flaw, every imperfection, every human limitation. That method belongs to an earlier stage of evolution — a stage where pressure and self‑punishment were used as motivation.

On the transfigurative path, that era is over.

Here, so‑called “flaws” are no longer interpreted as signs of weakness, but as **learning potentials** — precise indicators of where consciousness is ready to expand. Every limitation is an opening. Every reaction is a signal. Every struggle is a doorway through which light is trying to enter.

Transfiguration is not about becoming flawless.  
It is about becoming **available** to transformation.

When this shift settles into the subconscious, something remarkable happens:

- Guilt dissolves.
- Shame loses its grip.
- Self‑judgment gives way to curiosity.
- And consciousness begins to transform itself from within.
    
This is the essence of the transfigurative way:  
not to fight what arises, but to welcome it as the raw material of awakening.

It is here that the pilgrim becomes visible — not as a perfected personality, but as a being who has stopped resisting their own evolution and begun to cooperate with it.
